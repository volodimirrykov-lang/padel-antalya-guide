What is padel?

Padel is a racket sport played two against two on a small enclosed court with glass walls that keep the ball in play. It borrows tennis scoring, squash walls and an underhand serve — a mix that makes the first game fun with zero experience. That is why it is the fastest-growing racket sport in the world, and it is growing fast in Türkiye too.

What is padel in simple words?

A doubles racket sport on a 10x20 m court surrounded by glass and mesh. You score exactly like tennis (15/30/40, games, sets), serve underhand, and the ball may bounce off the walls and stay in play. Rallies are long, running is short, and beginners enjoy their very first game.

Is padel the same as paddle tennis or pickleball?

No — three different sports. Padel uses an enclosed court with walls, a solid perforated racket and a low-pressure tennis-like ball. Pickleball is played on an open court with a plastic ball and no walls. 'Paddle tennis' (POP tennis) is an older American open-court game. If you saw an enclosed glass court on Instagram — that is padel.

Why is padel so popular?

Three reasons: the first game is already fun (underhand serve, walls forgive mistakes), it is social by design (always four players, formats like americano rotate partners), and one court hour split four ways is affordable. Padel is widely reported as the world's fastest-growing racket sport, with tens of millions of players globally.

Is padel popular in Türkiye?

Yes and growing: courts exist in Istanbul, Ankara, İzmir, Antalya, Alanya and along the coast. Antalya has one of the country's most active scenes — including a standing weekly tournament calendar at V7 Padel Antalya (Konyaaltı), where ratings run on the international racket.id system. Do I need lessons to start playing padel?

No — most people start with an open game and learn as they play. A lesson or two speeds things up (grip, wall play, positioning), but the underhand serve and small court mean you can rally from day one. Clubs rent rackets and balls, so you need nothing to try it.
